Visual assumptions

Main slabs are shown at an assumed 150 mm and main-storey clear walls at 2.85 m. Interior doors use an assumed 2.10 m height unless scheduled. Side-window sills/heights are inferred: generally 1.00 / 1.20 m, with a high-level bathroom window. Furniture heights, joinery, material textures, frame depths, rail sections, planting, boundary heights and car styling are illustrative. Thin surface skins use a 3 mm rendering offset to avoid flicker; they do not revise the dimensioned envelope. The only structure shown is the conceptual RCC stair: an indicative 150 mm waist, 150 mm landing slabs and two support zones drawn inside walls and slab edges that already exist. No reinforcement, bar size, spacing, beam size, column size or footing is implied, and no other beams or columns have been invented.

Details awaiting coordination in the source

The plot is a nominal 9.70 × 16.80 m rectangle; the surveyed plot is slightly skewed. Parking turning clearance, exact bearing, well and septic siting, the final stair waist thickness, landing beams, supports and connections, guard anchors, cladding build-up, drainage falls, waterproofing and services remain unresolved. The services box is a reserve, not an installed tank.

Drawing conflicts made explicit

The rear ground service door is positioned at y = 8.20 m by the floor plan, although the elevation projects it to the rear façade. It is modeled in the recessed wall. The first-floor north bedroom has no opening in its long passage wall in the source, so its access is retained through the front gallery. The roof plan places both enclosure vents at x = 0.55 m; the rear elevation implies a 0.20 m lateral shift. The model follows the roof plan. The 900 mm roof-exit leaf swings into the indicated 1.10 m landing reserve; that reserve is not an additional raised platform.

This is a design-review visualization. The source is not a signed construction set, so an error-free or construction-certified model cannot be claimed.
R13 · LOW TV JOINERY / R12 ARCHITECTURE RETAINED

Compact fitted furniture beneath the staircase

R13 changes no riser, flight, landing, trimmer, beam zone or support condition, no basin, standing zone, mirror or archway — and nothing about the wall either. It changes only what stands in the volume under the flight. The R10/R10a three-bay stepped wedge is withdrawn, and so is the R10b proposal to take the stair-side partition out full height; the R11 relocation study is set aside. In their place: an 80 mm oak backing panel that fills the usable triangle under the single visible flight, flat at +2.55 m to y = 4.21 and then one straight rake to +1.88 m at the archway jamb, held 60 mm clear under the raking waist; a long low base cabinet, 1.95 × 0.50 m, capped at +0.45 m on a recessed lit plinth; a 55-inch screen surface-mounted on the panel on the approved y = 4.125 centreline, centre +1.02 m; three stepped open oak boxes in the low end of the wedge; and two floating oak shelves at the tall end. Nothing projects past the living wall line at x = 2.15.

Conceptual RCC stair structure

STAIR SHOWN AS CONCEPTUAL RCC WAIST-SLAB SYSTEM. FINAL WAIST-SLAB THICKNESS, LANDING BEAMS, SUPPORT CONDITIONS, REINFORCEMENT AND CONNECTIONS TO BE DESIGNED BY STRUCTURAL ENGINEER. 100 MM PARTITION WALLS ARE NOT TO BE ASSUMED LOAD-BEARING.

The massing shown is a folded plate: an indicative 150 mm waist under each 900 mm flight, its top plane on the steps' internal corners, monolithic with 150 mm landing slabs at the turns, with the 20 mm stone finish as a separate layer over the concrete so the walking levels never move. Support is taken only from elements that already exist — the plinth under the starter landing, the 150 mm west external wall, a landing beam zone inside the existing 100 mm bedroom cross-wall line, and the floor-slab trimmer at the stairwell edge (moved by R9 from y = 3.45 to y = 4.2). There are no cantilevered treads, no steel stringers, no wall below the upper flight and no new column in the living area. Thicknesses are conceptual massing only; no reinforcement, bar size, spacing, beam size, column size or footing is implied. The FF-to-roof stair keeps its own R6 system unchanged.

Riser shift: starter grows to 5, upper flight shrinks to 4

The route stays SOUTH → WEST → EAST. The starter flight grows from 2 to 5 risers, using the ~0.75 m of floor between the old first riser and the TV/closing-panel wall — floor that sat inside the stair's own footprint, not on any circulation route. The west flight keeps its exact R2 tread positions, renumbered 3 risers higher (6–12), so the intermediate landing rises from riser 9 to riser 12. The upper return flight now needs only 4 risers instead of 7 to close the same 3.00 m rise, at the same 250 mm going, so its run shrinks from 1.75 m to 1.00 m. The freed 0.75 m becomes extra first-floor landing/gallery floor at the top of the stair, and the floor-slab trimmer moves from y = 3.45 to y = 4.2. All 17 risers stay 176.47 mm; nothing about the first-to-roof stair changes.

What is done to the wall — and what is not

The 100 mm stair-side partition is cut away below +2.10 m over the 2.00 m the composition occupies, y = 3.20–5.20, and retained above as a plastered header to the 2.85 m slab soffit. That headed opening is the coordinated R10 condition; it is what sheet 11 and the framing data are drawn to, and R13 holds it unchanged. The partition is never assumed load-bearing and the stair-side trimmer zone already spans that whole wall line at the first-floor slab, but the opening still needs the same confirmation the earlier revisions require.

A full-height removal is not assumed and must not be inferred from the joinery. Taking the wall out floor to soffit with no header would show the whole flight in profile — the reference read — but it has not been verified structurally or architecturally, so it is not drawn. The consequence is honest and visible: from the living room you see the composition and the low end of the raking waist soffit under a 0.75 m header. The low backing exposes the lower flight beyond, while part of the upper return stays concealed. Because that header stands from +2.10 m to the slab and the flight's tread tops run +2.29 to +2.82 m, the east side of the flight is enclosed by the wall and no balustrade is required or drawn on it. R10 also closed a gap left by R9: the first-floor slab opening stops at y = 4.20, matching the arrival landing R9 extended to that line, so the composition has a real 2.85 m slab soffit over its north bays.

Basin against the bedroom (west) wall, facing east

The basin moves from the sloped soffit at y = 4.35–4.70 to sit flush against the bedroom wall at y = 5.75–6.10. plan-data.json's own coordinate note fixes y as increasing west, so this wall is the west wall and the basin — mounted against it, opening into the room — faces EAST, as requested; the user stands facing WEST toward the wall and mirror to use it. It now sits entirely under the flat landing slab raised to riser 12, so clear height is a near-uniform 1.91–1.95 m instead of the R6 1.73–2.40 m sloped range — short of the 2.20 m benchmark used elsewhere in this design, but close to stand-up height and a real gain over R6. The mirror mounts directly on the bedroom wall in place of the old mirror partition. The under-landing store this replaces (2.6 m² / 3.5 m³) is removed and not relocated. Access is a direct archway at its own position, not a walk-through behind the TV panel.

Bedroom 3 wardrobe

The original cabinet stays at its existing position. A 550 mm deep, 2.10 m high oak return runs along the adjoining entrance-side wall. Paired corner doors open without a fixed corner post. The entrance door, north window, bed and bedroom dimensions remain unchanged.

Front charcoal bands

The first-floor ribbon now has the reference's open upper-left termination and a separate lower return at the balcony. Both bands use custom cubic curves with broad, asymmetric sweeps and a slimmer 145 mm visible face. Their 80 mm visual projection is retained. Neither band relocates an opening, railing or slab.
